A plausible structure. A credible material?

A generated structure is a proposal, not yet a verified material. Our project connects three stages: MatterGen (matter jen) generates candidates, MACE (mace) predicts energies and forces, and independent physics checks selected predictions. We use existing models and archived project runs. The goal is to make each stage, and its limits, inspectable.

One crystal, three coupled variables

A crystal has three coupled ingredients. Atomic identities tell us what occupies each site. Fractional coordinates locate those sites inside the cell, and the lattice defines the repeating box. The highlighted site connects the picture to its numerical representation. These shapes distinguish a list of identities, a coordinate matrix, and a lattice matrix; they are different kinds of information.

Neighbours extend beyond the cell

The cell boundary is a bookkeeping boundary, not the edge of the material. An atom can have neighbours in adjacent periodic copies. The graph carries these relationships into the network, so messages depend on the local environment across cell boundaries, rather than only on distances within the visible box.

Different variables need different noise

The three variables do not share one generic noise process. Atomic identities can become MASK (mask), an unresolved category. Coordinates are perturbed within a periodic representation, while the lattice changes through its own continuous process. Isolating these effects makes the corruption process readable. This is an algorithm illustration, not molecular motion.
